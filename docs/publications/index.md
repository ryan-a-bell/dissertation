      abstract = {As Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed within the systems engineering domain, optimizing these models to balance computational efficiency, cost, and performance accuracy becomes essential. One process for balancing efficiency and accuracy for LLMs is quantization, a process that involves converting model parameters from higher precision formats to lower precision formats. This paper presents an empirical study investigating the impact of quantization at varying levels --- 4-bit, 8-bit, 16-bit, and 32-bit floating-point --- on LLM performance specifically within systems engineering contexts. The benchmark for systems engineering tasks, SysEngBench, will be used to evaluate LLM performance. SysEngBench is tailored to the systems engineering domain specific concepts such as requirements, architecture, model-based systems engineering, technical management processes, and specialty engineering activities. By examining accuracy loss across quantization levels, we can glean insight into scenarios where lower bit quantization provides efficiency gains with minimal impact on accuracy as well as scenarios where higher bit quantization levels are paramount for maintaining high fidelity responses. Analysis will be presented on quantization levels versus accuracy versus model size. Results will reveal distinct trade-offs that can guide practitioners in selecting appropriate quantization levels for their desired performance, cost, and resource requirements, which is particularly useful for model deployment in computation-constrained environments.},

      abstract = {In the systems engineering (SE) community, generative artificial intelligence (GenAI), such as large language models (LLMs), continues to grow in popularity with applications varying from help with writing requirements, to creating traditional text-based documents, and to generating models. Furthermore, the text-based format of the Systems Modeling Language version 2 (SysMLv2) gives rise to wide ranging possibilities when combined with LLMs. It is common to specialize LLMs on specific tasks, such as SE; which may be achieved by a number of means. Fine-tuning and retrieval augmented generation (RAG) are example methods to teaching a model how to act and what the model needs to know, respectively. Typical practice evaluates LLMs relative to one another through the use of benchmarks. Benchmarks are tasks that use a common scoring-based method where the LLM either answers a set of multiple-choice questions or textual queries to verified, correct answers. A domain specific benchmark for SE, SysEngBench, was developed over the last year and will be leveraged for the domain specific needs of SE evaluation. With many in the SE community developing custom language models, the usage of a standard benchmark is essential for relative performance comparison. We have conducted a limited experiment using SysEngBench to compare performance between ChatGPT4o, fine-tuned ChatGPT4o, and fine-tuned ChatGPT4o with RAG; results being what many in the SE community may find intuitively unexpected.},
