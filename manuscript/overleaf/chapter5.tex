This study was scoped to evaluate large language models (LLMs) for systems engineering tasks through comparative \ac{mcq} and \ac{osq} benchmarking, including hybrid approaches and tokenomics analysis. The work is anchored in foundational systems engineering concepts drawn from the INCOSE Handbook and related activities such as requirements analysis, conceptual design, risk management, and model-based systems engineering. The overarching goal is to make SysEngBench the definitive benchmarking resource for systems engineering, supplying a methodological template for both its ongoing development and for those creating private or derivative benchmarks, and to guide LLM integration into SE workflows.

The research is deliberately bounded in both concept and method. It examines model performance and evaluation modalities rather than downstream system builds, hardware prototyping, or full lifecycle automation. The analysis focuses on frontier and small LLMs. Benchmarks are confined to MCQ and OSQ formats and their hybrid combinations; interactive dialogues, multimodal, or agentic inputs remain outside the present scope. From a data and temporal perspective, the study relies on SysEngBench content drawn from publicly available systems engineering sources and does not incorporate proprietary industrial datasets. The technology snapshot reflects the 2024 – late-2025 LLM landscape, recognizing that future models may shift absolute performance while leaving the analytical framework relevant.

Several limitations qualify the findings. Automated scoring of \acp{osq} combines algorithmic metrics with language model as a judge, introducing some subjectivity. Tokenomics analysis focuses on token counts and inference latency to highlight cost-modeling trade-offs, rather than offering a full economic forecast such as dollars per million tokens, especially as inference costs continue to compress with large-scale adoption. In terms of generalizability, the conclusions are most applicable to systems engineering and may not fully extend to highly specialized engineering subfields or non-engineering disciplines. Finally, SysEngBench is designed for extensibility, and thus this dissertation documents only its initial large-scale iteration; subsequent updates will be needed to capture future advances in \ac{llm} capabilities.
